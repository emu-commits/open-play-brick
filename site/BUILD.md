# Build an Open Play Brick

This is the module build: ready-made breakout boards on a breadboard, around an Adafruit ESP32-C6 Feather. No surface-mount soldering and no custom circuit board.

## Read this first

- **Nobody has built this yet.** The firmware runs in the [emulator](index.html) and compiles for the ESP32-C6, but it has not run on real hardware and has not read a real tag. If you build one you are the first tester. Please [open an issue](https://github.com/emu-commits/open-play-brick/issues) with what happens, good or bad.
- **It is not brick-sized.** The boards together need a space about 75 x 50 x 30 mm, roughly a 10 x 7 stud box three bricks tall. A version the size of a real 2 x 4 brick needs a custom circuit board that has not been designed. The [roadmap](https://github.com/emu-commits/open-play-brick/blob/main/docs/ROADMAP.md) explains the path to one.
- **It needs some soldering**: plain header pins on the boards, about 70 joints, an hour for a beginner. There is no version with none.
- **An adult should build it.** It uses a lithium battery and small parts. Give children the finished, enclosed thing.
- It reads the same tags as the original and plays in the same spirit, with its own sounds. It does not play LEGO's sounds, and it does not talk to LEGO's bricks or app.

Tools: a soldering iron and solder, wire cutters, a small screwdriver, and a computer with Chrome or Edge.

Notes on the two parts that are not from Adafruit:

- **PN5180 reader.** Sold by Elechouse and by many sellers on Amazon, eBay and AliExpress. Any board with the PN5180 chip and a printed antenna works. Pad names vary: see step 6.
- **Colour sensor.** Search for "TCS34725 module". Pick one with a white LED on the board and a pad marked LED.

## Steps

Work in stages. After each stage the brick tells you over USB whether the new part is alive, so a mistake shows up while there is only one thing it could be.

### 1. Solder the headers

Solder the pin headers to the Feather, the amplifier, the microphone, the reader (if its pins came loose) and the colour sensor. On the accelerometer you only need one pin, **INT**. Solder three wires or a 3-pin header to the pixel stick's **DIN**, **5VDC** and **GND** pads on the input end.

### 2. Flash the firmware

Do this before wiring anything, with only the Feather plugged into USB.

1. Open the [build page](build.html) in Chrome or Edge on a computer.
2. Click **Install firmware** and choose the port named "USB JTAG/serial debug unit".
3. When it finishes, click **Logs & console** in the same dialog.

You should see this. The errors are expected: nothing is connected yet.

```
Open Play Brick firmware 0.2.0
Catalogue: 9 tags, 11 profiles, ...
ERROR: PN5180 not responding
ERROR: LIS3DH not responding
ERROR: TCS3472x not responding
MAX17048 found, version 0x0012
Waiting for a tag (ISO 15693)...
```

If the port does not appear, hold the Feather's **Boot** button, tap **Reset**, release Boot, and try again.

Unplug USB before each wiring step below, and plug it back in to check.

### 3. Accelerometer

Plug a STEMMA QT cable from the Feather's socket into either socket on the LIS3DH board. Run one jumper from the LIS3DH **INT** pad to Feather **A0**.

Check: `LIS3DH found` replaces the error. Shake the board and you should see `Shake with no tag`.

### 4. Colour sensor

Plug the QT-to-pins cable into the LIS3DH board's second socket. On the colour sensor: red to **VIN**, black to **GND**, blue to **SDA**, yellow to **SCL**. Run a jumper from its **LED** pad to Feather **TX**.

Check: `TCS3472x found`, and the sensor's white LED goes dark a moment after power-up. It lights only while a tag is on the brick.

### 5. Amplifier and speaker

Wire the amplifier as in the table: **BCLK** to **A3**, **LRC** to **A2**, **DIN** to **7**, **SD** to **RX**, **Vin** to **BAT**, **GND** to **GND**. Screw the two speaker wires into the terminal block.

Check: a three-note chime at power-up.

### 6. NFC reader

Six signal wires and power, as in the table. Two things depend on which board you have:

- If it has both a **5V** and a **3.3V** pad, connect 5V to Feather **BAT** and 3.3V to Feather **3V**.
- If it has a 5V pad and the 3.3V pad is an output (the Elechouse board), connect only 5V to Feather **BAT**, and close its solder jumper for 3.3 V logic if it has one.

Running the reader's transmitter from the battery instead of 5 V is within the chip's rating but gives less range than its maker quotes. If tags only read when pressed right against the antenna, that is the first thing to suspect: see Troubleshooting.

Check: `PN5180 product 4.0 firmware 4.0 eeprom 153.0` (your numbers may differ). Then hold a tag flat on the antenna:

```
ISO 15693 tag detected, UID E0:16:5C:01:...
  Block 0: 00 6B 01 0C
  Payload length 107 bytes (blocks 0-26), CRC-32 0x90A404C8
  => X-Wing (item), exact CRC match
```

This is the moment that proves the idea. If you get this far, please report it.

### 7. Pixels

With the MOSFET board: its 3-pin cable goes **signal** to Feather **RX** (the same pad as the amplifier's SD), **V+** to **BAT**, **GND** to **GND**. The stick's **5VDC** goes to the MOSFET board's power terminal and the stick's **GND** to its switched terminal. The stick's **DIN** goes to Feather **14**.

Without the MOSFET board: stick **5VDC** to **BAT**, **GND** to **GND**, **DIN** to **14**. Everything works the same, but the stick draws current even when dark, so unplug the battery when you put the brick away.

Check: a rainbow sweep at power-up, then a slow dim white pulse.

### 8. Microphone

**3V** to Feather **3V**, **GND** to **GND**, **BCLK** to **A3** and **LRCL** to **A2** (shared with the amplifier), **DOUT** to **A1**. Leave **SEL** unconnected, or tie it to GND if your board needs it for the left channel.

Check: clap once near it and you should see `Clap with no tag` and hear a two-note reply.

### 9. Battery

Plug the battery into the Feather. It charges whenever USB is connected. If your battery is not from Adafruit, check that its red wire lines up with the **+** mark by the socket before plugging in: some brands are wired the other way round and will damage the board.

Check: unplug USB and it keeps running. Leave it still for 30 seconds with no tag and it goes to sleep; pick it up and it wakes.

## What it should do when finished

| Do this | It should |
| --- | --- |
| Put a tag on the antenna | Play that tag's sound, light in its colour, switch the white lamp on |
| Shake, swing, tilt, turn over, knock or toss it | A different sound and light pattern for each |
| Hold something red, green or blue in front of the colour sensor | Fire, repair or refuel sounds |
| Clap | A short reply |
| Take the tag off | A goodbye sound, lamp off |
| Leave it alone for 30 seconds | Sleep. Moving it wakes it |

Try all of these in the [emulator](index.html) first: it runs the same firmware.

## Housing

There is no case design yet. The simplest housing is one built from your own bricks: a hollow box with the reader's antenna lying flat directly under a lid of plates or tiles. A tag reads through one plate. Keep the speaker and the colour sensor at openings, the pixels behind transparent bricks, and metal away from the antenna. Measure your boards first: reader modules come in several sizes.

## Adding your own tags

The firmware knows the nine tags listed in [tags.json](data/tags.json). Any other play tag still works, with a generic sound, and the console prints the two numbers that identify it:

```
=> not in the catalogue. Add len 0x55, crc 0x1234ABCD to tags.json.
```

Add an entry to `site/data/tags.json` with those numbers, a name, a colour and one of the sound profiles, then rebuild and flash the catalogue. This step needs the command line for now:

```sh
node tools/build_catalog.mjs
parttool.py --port /dev/ttyACM0 write_partition --partition-name catalog --input build/catalog.bin
```

Please also send the numbers in an issue or pull request so the next builder gets them for free.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| `ERROR: ... not responding` for one board | That board's power, ground or data wires. For the two sensors, the QT cable. |
| Both sensors and `MAX17048` missing | SDA and SCL swapped on the colour sensor: a stuck I2C bus takes the whole bus down. Unplug it and see if the others return. |
| `PN5180 not responding` | SCK, MOSI, MISO, NSS, BUSY or RST on the wrong pad. Check that the board has power on both its supply pads if it needs both. |
| Reader found, tags never detected | Hold the tag flat and centred on the antenna, touching. If it then reads, range is the problem: power the reader's 5V pad from a 5 V boost board (Adafruit 4654) instead of BAT. Keep the battery and speaker away from the antenna. |
| No sound | Speaker wires, or SD not connected to RX. |
| Sound but distorted or very quiet | Battery low, or the amplifier's Vin is on 3V instead of BAT. |
| Pixels dark | DIN on the wrong end of the stick (use the end marked DIN, not DOUT), or the MOSFET board's signal wire not on RX. |
| Claps not heard | DOUT not on A1, or the microphone is set to the right channel: tie SEL to GND. |
| Claps "heard" constantly | Microphone touching the speaker. Move them apart. |
| Does not wake when moved | INT not connected to A0. |
| Battery flat after two or three days unused | No MOSFET board: the pixel stick draws about 8 mA even when dark. |

For anything else, copy the console output into an issue.

## Battery life

Estimates from datasheet figures, not measurements: about 5 hours of continuous play (3 with a vehicle tag, which keeps the radio on), 10 hours awake but idle (it sleeps after 30 seconds, so this rarely matters), and about three weeks asleep with the MOSFET board fitted.
